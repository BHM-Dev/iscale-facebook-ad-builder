# Auto Insurance — Video Creative Guidance for AI Video Agent

Source: Ad Builder Research Library, legacy "Auto insurance" vertical (`59242e13-fa01-4052-9409-c6cfbb2d0802`), imported 2026-03-05 to 2026-03-16. 23 saved ads across 13 advertiser pages, pulled directly from the DB via the Research API (the current Research page tabs point at a newer, empty "Auto Insurance" vertical created 2026-07-17 — these 23 ads aren't visible in the UI right now, only via API).

This is guidance **for our video agent**, not copy to lift. No headline, body copy, or brand name below should be reproduced verbatim in a BHM ad.

---

## 1. Top 5 Hook Patterns

1. **Overpay callout** — direct 2nd/1st-person accusation that the viewer is paying too much. ("I know you're overpaying for car insurance." / "I hate dealing with insurance — but 2 minutes to get money back? I can handle that.") Works because it names the exact frustration before asking for anything.
2. **Hidden-thing curiosity** — implies most people don't know a savings mechanism exists, paired with artificial scarcity. ("Most American car owners don't know this exists" / "See before it's taken down.") High CTR pattern but leans on manufactured urgency — use sparingly and don't fake a takedown deadline that isn't real.
3. **Long-tenure surprise narrative** — a driver with a clean record for 10-25+ years discovers they've been overpaying the whole time. Strong because it defeats the "I already have a good rate" objection before it's raised.
4. **Geo-localized relevance** — "Drivers across [STATE] are reviewing their rates this year" — same script, state swapped per ad set. Cheap to scale, works because local specificity reads as "this is about me," but it was the weakest-effort creative in the set (identical video, one word changed) — treat as a scaling tactic, not a hook to imitate line-for-line.
5. **Concrete before/after number** — a specific monthly or annual dollar swing stated early and often ($147 → $47/mo, save up to $637/yr). Numbers this specific outperform vague "save money" language — but every claim needs a substantiation basis before it goes live (see Section 3).

## 2. Top 5 Visual/Video Structures Worth Imitating

Media URLs had expired on the saved records (Meta CDN links die within hours), so these are inferred from copy structure, on-screen text patterns, and run length — confirm visually by opening the Ad Library links in Section 6 before final sign-off.

1. **Direct-to-camera monologue, 20-40 sec** — matches the "I've been driving for 25 years..." / "I hate dealing with insurance" scripts. Single person, phone-shot framing, talking through the realization → action arc. This is the highest-value structure to imitate for our UGC actor pipeline.
2. **Screen-recording rate reveal** — a phone or browser screen showing a quote form being filled and a rate appearing, voiced over "see your new rate in 90 seconds." Works well paired with hook #1.
3. **Before/after number card** — a mid-roll graphic card showing old rate crossed out next to new rate ($147 → $47), 2-3 seconds on screen, matches the Llama-style ad. Strong for the numeric hook.
4. **Text-card + b-roll loop, 15-30 sec** — state-name or headline text overlaid on generic driving/road b-roll, looping. Cheapest to produce, matches the Auto Best geo-scaling pattern — fine as a volume/testing format, not the hero format.
5. **Discount-banner overlay on short-form video** — persistent emoji/dollar banner ("💸Save up to $X/yr💸") stacked over a 30-sec video. Useful as a paid-social bumper format but reads discount-y/low-trust at this density — dial back emoji count for BHM's brand bar.

## 3. CTA Language We Can Safely Use

Everything below avoids unverifiable dollar guarantees, avoids "free money"/"you're owed" framing, and avoids fake scarcity — all three showed up in the saved set and are compliance risks (FTC endorsement guides + state insurance ad rules).

**Safe, ready to use:**
- "Check Your Rate"
- "See If You Qualify"
- "Compare Free Quotes"
- "See Your Rate in Under a Minute"
- "Get Your Free Quote"
- "Start Your Quote"
- "Find Your Rate"

**Usable with a substantiation basis on file (real data behind the number):**
- "As Low As $[X]/mo" — only if $X reflects an actual live quote floor we can point to
- "Compare Rates From Top Carriers" — only if the destination genuinely multi-quotes
- "See How Much You Could Save" — vague-savings framing is safer than a fixed dollar figure and needs no substantiation

**Do not use:**
- Any fixed savings guarantee ("Save $637/yr") without a disclosed, defensible basis and asterisked disclaimer
- "You may be owed $$$" / compensation-style language — that's a legal lead-gen pattern, not insurance, and reads as bait
- Fake urgency ("before it's taken down," countdown timers with no real expiration)
- Any cloaked/lookalike-character text (see Section 5) — several saved ads used Cyrillic lookalike letters to evade ad-text scanners; this is a platform-ban risk, not just a style choice

## 5. Reject/Redo Rubric — AI Video Artifacts

Use as a hard gate before any AI-generated video/actor asset goes to review. Any single "Reject" = redo, no exceptions — these are the failure modes that get ads disapproved or destroy trust in the 3-second window before a skip.

| Check | Pass | Reject (redo) |
|---|---|---|
| **On-screen text** | Real words, correctly spelled, standard characters | Garbled text, mismatched kerning, or lookalike/cloaked characters (e.g. Cyrillic а/е/о substituted for Latin) — several saved ads in this vertical did this to dodge ad-text scanners; never replicate it |
| **Steering wheel / hands** | Wheel geometry consistent, hands plausibly gripping, correct number of fingers | Warped wheel shape, hands merging into wheel, extra/missing fingers, wheel side inconsistent with dashboard layout |
| **Phone/UI mockups** | Realistic OS chrome, plausible app layout, legible and correctly-spelled on-screen copy | Fake/generic UI that reads as obviously AI (impossible button layouts, illegible or garbled in-app text, notification bars that don't match any real OS) |
| **Lip sync** | Mouth shapes track the audio within ~1 frame, consistent through the full line | Mouth movement drifts from audio, "guppy mouth" (movement without corresponding phonemes), sync breaks on plosives (b/p/m sounds) |
| **Actor/voice match** | Voice timbre, accent, and energy plausibly belong to the pictured person; consistent across cuts | Voice doesn't match apparent age/gender/energy of actor, or shifts mid-video; actor's face doesn't match their own voice's mouth shapes |
| **Continuity** | Same actor, clothing, background, and lighting across all cuts in one ad | Actor's face, hairstyle, or setting subtly shifts between cuts (classic AI-frame tell) |
| **Hands/limbs generally** | Correct anatomy at rest and in motion | Extra digits, merging limbs, unnatural bends — common failure point during hand gestures |
| **Background objects** | Stable, physically plausible | Warping signage, melting furniture, text on background objects (street signs, labels) that's garbled |

## 7. Video Format Strategy — Tight Talking-Head + Flexible B-Roll (not either/or)

This came out of the video harness testing (2026-08-02/03): AI video generation hallucinates fake on-screen text/UI graphics more when the shot is wide or complex (car doors, background panels, signage). The instinct was to fix this by shrinking every shot to tight chest-up/interior-only framing — but that also kills the visual variety (someone walking, driving, getting out of the car) that makes a video library feel like a real ad set instead of one repeated shot.

**The resolution: these aren't in tension once you split them by clip type.**

- **Talking-head segments (the primary, voiced, lip-synced clip) should default to tight chest-up/interior framing** — not purely as a hallucination workaround, but because it's the stronger creative choice on its own merits. The highest-performing examples pulled from competitor research (Dorothy Hawkins' "$157→$39/mo," the Llama/Georgina Jacobs ad, Brian Newman's testimonial) are all tight, phone-camera-style, in-car or selfie framing. That's what makes UGC read as *authentically UGC* — a wide, cinematically-blocked shot undercuts the "just a regular person talking" credibility the format depends on. Tight framing is also lower hallucination-risk, so both the creative and reliability arguments point the same direction here.
- **B-roll segments (short, silent cutaways) are where the flexibility belongs.** Walking to the car, getting in, driving away, a quick establishing shot — these are shorter (the harness already runs broll at ~8s vs. 15s for talking-head), carry no lip-sync/audio-sync dependency, and are cheap to discard if a generation comes back odd. This is the right place to spend the "diverse UGC" budget, layered around the tight talking-head core rather than replacing it.

**Practical implication for the harness config:** don't force every format's `broll_prompt` into the same tight-interior mold as the talking-head clips — that over-corrects. Keep talking-head tight by default; let broll formats vary (walking, driving, exterior shots) since a bad 8-second silent cutaway is a much cheaper mistake than a bad 15-second voiced segment with hallucinated text baked into it.
